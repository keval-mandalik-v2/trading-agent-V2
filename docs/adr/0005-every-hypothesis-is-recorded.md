# Every hypothesis tested against the Journal is recorded, including the failures

Forty-three variants were tested against a single set of 88 Signals while designing this
system — coil definitions, exit rules, fill assumptions, selection rules, sizing rules,
decision protocols and factor tests. A permutation test showed that any one variant had
roughly a 1-in-9 chance of appearing profitable through luck alone, so 43 tests should
yield four or five false winners. Exactly four appeared. Without a count of what was tried,
those four are indistinguishable from discoveries.

We therefore keep a research log recording every hypothesis ever evaluated against the
Journal, its result, and the date — failures included. Any claimed edge must be reported
together with the number of variants tested to reach it.

## Consequences

- The honest interpretation of a result depends on a number stored outside that result. A
  finding quoted without its trial count is not a finding.
- Deleting or forgetting a failed test silently inflates every future claim, so the log is
  append-only.
- None of the four apparent winners found during design may be treated as validated. They
  are hypotheses awaiting out-of-sample evidence from the Journal.
